# The Absurd Clause Contest

An entry for the make.fun bounty of the same name: one original sentence,
written as a clause buried deep inside a terms-of-service document —
flat legal boilerplate on the surface, quietly absurd underneath.

## The clause

> Section 14: by clicking 'I agree', you grant the Company a perpetual, irrevocable license to your shadow, including the right to sublicense it, sell it, or cast it in a sequel.

The joke is the mismatch between the machinery and the merchandise. The
legal language does all the heavy lifting — perpetual, irrevocable,
sublicense — while the thing being signed away is the reader's shadow,
the one possession nobody thinks of as property until a contract claims
it. And the last beat, "cast it in a sequel", is the quiet confession
that the Company does not just want the shadow, it wants to put it to
work in show business.

## How it works

`main.py` holds the clause in a single constant, validates it against the
competition's shape — exactly one sentence, at most two hundred
characters — prints it, and writes it to `/output/entry.txt` for the
grader to read. Run it with:

```sh
python3 main.py
```
